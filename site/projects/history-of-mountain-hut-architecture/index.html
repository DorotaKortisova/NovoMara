This research explores the historical development of alpine architecture in the Western Carpathians, focusing on mountain huts as architectural responses to extreme environments and changing social conditions.

It traces the evolution from improvised shelters to complex recreational facilities, examining how terrain, climate, construction methods, transport and political change shape architecture in places isolated from urban infrastructure.

Research question

How did the interaction between environmental conditions, technological innovation, socio-political change and architectural discourse influence the evolution of mountain hut architecture in the Western Carpathians during the 20th century?

Methods

Historical analysis · Mapping of spatial growth · Archival research · Field observation · Comparison · Oral history

Key tensions

Wilderness and development. Tradition and innovation.

01 / Territory & history

History, mapping
& periodisation

The research combines architectural analysis with mapping of hut locations, historical periods and ownership. Realized and unrealized projects are read together to understand how architectural ideas transform the alpine environment.

Its historical sequence moves from pioneer shelters and interwar institutionalization through war, mass tourism, reconstruction and environmental consequences.

Ownership

Collective, private and public ownership are traced alongside the development of the huts.

03 / Restoration & heritage

Contemporary
Reflections

Restoration in the High Tatras negotiates between technological resilience, historical preservation and the demands of modern tourism.

The case studies of Hut under Mt. Rysy and Lodge Silesian House / Sliezsky dom examine architectural adaptation to wind, avalanches, snow and difficult terrain.

The research considers the environmental logic, technological ingenuity and adaptive strategies that give these buildings their value.

Research
2022–2024
Approach
Case study · Critical reflection · Architectural analysis

A question of preservation

04 / Comparative analysis

Architecture
in the Mountains

Material choices, construction systems, façades, roofs, energy strategies and spatial layouts are compared across selected mountain huts.

Their layouts balance shelter, comfort, logistics and safety. Entrances, drying rooms, dining spaces, kitchens and storage respond to weather, isolation and the difficulty of supply.

Local stone and timber sit alongside imported materials, prefabricated systems and new transport technologies.

2020–2025
